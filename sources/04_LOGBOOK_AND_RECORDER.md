The first whole-EFB build targets the full approved Q1–Q14 Logbook, including genuine automatic Flight Logging through read-only MSFS 2020 SimConnect, manual entry and manual override. The illustrated Logbook Design Record v1.2 remains the detail reference. Advanced layout customisation retains its C0.2 deferral.

The Recorder consumes Core observations, owns flight state and writes durable local records. The approved flight boundary is gate-to-gate, from pushback or taxi-out through parking after arrival. Track airborne time separately, from takeoff to touchdown. Exact event detection and thresholds, simulator clock mappings and discontinuity handling, multiple-sector handling, interruption, crash recovery and local record schema remain open. Retain gaps and provenance. Treat unsupported values as unavailable and never infer a pilot-confirmed diversion, airport, landing rate or payload without evidence.

The gate-to-gate rules must accommodate third-party pushback and parking tools. Corroborate proposed boundary events with aircraft movement and available telemetry such as on-ground state and flight phase. Evaluate engine start and shutdown as fallback clues that require corroboration. The exact signals, thresholds and timestamp quality need validation. Evaluate engine run time as a separate metric, per engine where Core can reliably observe it. A direct integration with any third-party tool requires separate scope approval.

Count repeated wheel contact during a bounce as one landing. A confidently identified go-around without touchdown is a Black Box event and leaves the flight open. Log each confirmed touch-and-go as a landing event in the same flight, which closes after the final arrival and parking. Show the final arrival's read-only landing rate in Flight History when a valid measurement exists. Keep earlier landing events and any valid measurements in the Black Box timeline, and show the final landing rate as unavailable if it could not be measured.

Show a separate touchdown-rate grade and an overall landing grade. Preserve the observed fpm as read-only evidence. Review pitch, bank, indicated airspeed, groundspeed, wind speed and direction, and peak G near touchdown. Include runway alignment, lateral drift, centreline offset, touchdown location and runway remaining where reliable. Show wind and its runway-relative components as context, and assess aircraft control in those conditions. Use aircraft-appropriate targets when available.

Grade speed against a verified target for that approach and phase. Prefer a reliable aircraft-reported target, and consider an aircraft-specific reference only when the landing weight, flap configuration and applicable wind or gust adjustments are known. Retain the target and its source. If no reliable target is available, show the measured airspeed and groundspeed and leave the speed assessment ungraded. [Airbus operating speeds](https://safetyfirst.airbus.com/app/themes/mh_newsdesk/pdf/safety_first_24.pdf)

Assess crosswind technique in the aircraft's context. Use available evidence about runway track versus heading, lateral drift, bank and yaw through the flare, wheel contact and directional control on rollout. Allow for aircraft-appropriate crab, de-crab and wing-low techniques. Leave technique unclassified when telemetry cannot distinguish it. A later approved FSUIPC integration may supply additional inputs through Core, subject to validation of their meaning and quality. [FAA approaches and landings](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook/10_afh_ch9.pdf) · [Aircraft and extension boundaries](06_AIRCRAFT_AND_EXTENSIONS.md)

Use timestamped telemetry sequences around the approach, flare, touchdown and initial rollout to infer behaviours where there is no direct event. Evaluate combinations of signals for float, ballooning, bounce, drift correction and potential go-around. For example, corroborate a brief loss of ground contact after touchdown with height and vertical-motion data before classifying a bounce. Keep observed measurements, inferred behaviours and derived grades distinct. Record the evidence supporting each inference and leave it unclassified when the sample cadence, continuity or aircraft support is insufficient. Validate detections with representative offline sequences and separately approved live comparisons before grading from them.

Proposed overall score for owner review: touchdown and contact sequence (30%), runway placement (30%), approach energy management (25%) and aircraft control (15%). Judge a float by time and distance before touchdown and where the aircraft lands. Distinguish ballooning where the evidence supports it. Keep one landing event for a bounce, with each known contact and the strongest measured impact available for review. Avoid penalising the same behaviour twice. Record a go-around separately and assess each touch-and-go within its parent flight. Show a short reason for each grade and leave a component or the overall result ungraded when required inputs are missing. Scoring bands, aircraft profiles, measurement windows and minimum evidence remain open. ### Landing telemetry research

These are proposed detectors for offline replay and later live validation. Build the evidence from a time-aligned sequence through final approach, flare, contact and initial rollout. Keep the indicated vertical-speed trend separate from the SDK's last-touchdown ground-normal speed. Validate sign, units, cadence, and whether a contact result belongs to the current landing before attaching it. Grade peak G only where the sample timing and source can resolve the contact. [MSFS aircraft variables](https://docs.flightsimulator.com/html/Programming_Tools/SimVars/Aircraft_SimVars/Aircraft_Misc_Variables.htm)

| Behaviour | Signals to evaluate |
|---|---|
| Float or long flare | Sustained low height above the runway with V/S flattening near zero while the aircraft remains airborne and covers ground. Measure time and along-runway distance, with threshold-to-touchdown and flare-to-touchdown kept distinct where available. Show speed trend and corroborate the touchdown point before calling it long. |
| Balloon | Sustained height gain and positive vertical motion after flare onset and before first contact. Use pitch trend as context and cross-check against runway-relative height or another altitude reference to reject terrain or slope artefacts. |
| Bounce or porpoise | Contact, a corroborated return to airborne, then another contact. Track the gaps, vertical motion and strongest reliably measured impact. Use repeated pitch reversals and alternating contact evidence as a possible porpoise pattern only where the aircraft exposes meaningful contact data. Distinguish a sustained climb and departure from a brief bounce and treat pilot intent as unknown unless identified. |
| Crosswind correction | Use wind speed and direction against the direction of the runway actually used, with all bearings on the same true-north basis. Derive headwind or tailwind and signed crosswind. Compare heading, ground track, change in centreline offset, bank and yaw through the flare and rollout. Assess the outcome in context and leave the technique unknown when ambiguous. |
| Runway use and rollout | Match the runway end using touchdown position and approach track. Measure distance past the usable threshold, centreline offset and runway remaining. Account for displaced thresholds, parallel runways and slope. Assess initial rollout tracking separately from normal runway exit. Leave runway-based scoring unavailable if runway identification or geometry is uncertain. |

Consider a landing replay in Flight Details showing the contact points on an along-runway strip, with aligned height, V/S, pitch and lateral track traces. Show the reasons behind each grade, plus evidence coverage. Evaluate a short, bounded higher-cadence sample around the landing through the shared Core session. Validate which rates capture brief events without undue simulator impact. Suggested replay cases include a soft but long touchdown, a controlled crosswind arrival, balloon before contact, bounce after contact, intentional touch-and-go, go-around, and missing or stale telemetry. [FAA approaches and landings](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook/10_afh_ch9.pdf) · [MSFS runway facilities](https://docs.flightsimulator.com/html/Programming_Tools/SimConnect/API_Reference/Facilities/SimConnect_AddToFacilityDefinition.htm) · [MSFS request cadence](https://docs.flightsimulator.com/html/Programming_Tools/SimConnect/API_Reference/Events_And_Data/SimConnect_RequestDataOnSimObject.htm)

Actual in-simulator delivery, whether the SDK retains touchdown values long enough for reliable capture, and the reliability of grading remain unverified. Use the real-world flight date as the primary Logbook date for History and Statistics. Show the simulator date separately in Flight Details when available. Use simulator time for flight event timestamps and elapsed metrics whenever reliably available. Gate-to-gate and airborne durations use elapsed simulator time, reflect sim-rate changes and exclude Pause and Active Pause. Keep real-world timestamps for record history and diagnostics. Expose missing timing and gaps rather than silently substituting wall-clock time. Validate simulator clock mappings, pause behaviour and discontinuities before accepting automatic durations.

Apply the shared Zulu or real-world local time display preference to Logbook timestamps. Retain each timestamp's original clock source. Convert simulator timestamps only when their time basis is known. Clearly label an unconvertible simulator time as such. Keep the simulator date distinct from the real-world Logbook date and preserve date-only manual entries.

On a SimConnect interruption during recording, persist the record so far, mark the gap and show Flight Logging as `Interrupted`. At reconnect, compare the last reliable observations with fresh Core telemetry. Use simulator date and time progression alongside aircraft identity, position and flight phase to assess continuity. Account for pause, sim rate and clock changes when interpreting elapsed simulator time. Resume the same record and retain the gap when continuity is established. If continuity is unclear, keep the partial record for review. Use reliable simulator timestamps to bound the gap when available. Leave missing events and measurements unknown. The exact continuity checks remain open.

When closing the EFB during active logging, offer `Cancel` and `Save & Exit`. Save the unfinished record durably before exiting and surface any save failure. After a crash or restart, recover the last durable checkpoint as `Interrupted`. If fresh telemetry establishes continuity with a running flight, offer `Resume flight` and retain the gap when the pilot resumes. Keep checkpoint and recovery logic independent of the Windows UI and the means of connecting to Core so future platforms or networking can reuse it. The first build uses local storage and connectivity. Checkpoint frequency, schema and recovery verification remain open.

Show the manual `Start Logging` and `Stop Logging` controls directly in the status footer whenever their actions are available. Manual Start can begin recording when automatic detection has missed the start. Manual Stop requires confirmation and durably saves the data captured so far. Do not automatically restart the same flight after a manual stop. Re-arm automatic logging for the next flight and allow the pilot to explicitly start logging again when eligible.

### Storage and telemetry preferences

The owner ruled out SQL for EFB storage, including SQLite. Use one human-readable JSON file per real-world flight year for manual, automatic and supported imported flight records. Provide CSV import and export. Store optional replay data separately in plain-text JSON Lines batches linked by flight ID. Start with a quarterly batch and evaluate monthly batches if replay size or lookup performance warrants it. Create a batch only when it has data to store. The exact schema and safe external editing workflow remain open.

Map supported CSV columns into the shared flight record. Preview imports with validation errors and likely duplicates before saving, and preserve the import source. Use a stable source record ID when it is available. Otherwise compare real-world flight dates and like-for-like timestamps, normalised to a common time basis when known. Corroborate with route, aircraft type, registration or flight number when available. Do not treat a date-only match or an unknown time basis as conclusive.

Keep one Logbook record and stable record ID per flight. Match reports and data from any supported source to the existing flight. For event-level data, also establish which event it belongs to before adding it. Preview eligible blanks that can be filled and let the pilot approve the additions. Preserve each added value's source and apply the pilot's storage preferences. Repeated imports of the same matched evidence must not create extra flights or duplicate additions. Flag conflicting values and ambiguous matches for review, and preserve existing read-only measurements. Do not interpret missing imported data as a native recording gap. Keep the import interface open to later support for VATSIM records and logbooks from other simulators and apps. Matching tolerances, supported formats, mappings and external access need separate review.

Use one local record format and storage path for manual and automatic flights, with source and correction history preserved. Let pilots choose how much information to retain, including optional record fields, event detail and raw telemetry or replay samples. Show what each choice affects and identify the minimum data needed for an enabled workflow. Let pilots disable automatic Flight Logging entirely. Keep manual entry and browsing saved records available independently of Live Flight Logging.

Keep collection and retention distinct. If an optional field is excluded from storage, the Recorder must not subscribe to it solely for storage. Request it through Core only while an enabled feature or module needs it. Share demand across consumers and stop requesting a field when the last consumer releases it. A feature may process needed data transiently without persisting it. Optional fields excluded by the pilot must stay out of automatic records, recovery checkpoints and routine application logs. Make any effect on replay, grading or recovery visible before the pilot changes the setting. Apply capture preferences to future writes and give pilots separate, explicit control over existing records. Exact storage schema and preference layout remain open.

Registration and flight number are optional, including for offline flights. Pilots can enter them manually. A later approved VATSIM integration may supply available identity fields with their source recorded. Show flight distance when supported by the record. How distance is calculated and how recording gaps affect it remain open.

Show `Planned distance` when the flight record has a planned-route distance. If it is missing, calculate the direct airport-to-airport distance from the departure and destination coordinates and label it `Direct distance`. If either airport coordinate is unavailable, show the distance as unavailable. Keep these values distinct from `Distance flown`, which describes the recorded flight.

Allow Logbook fields to be populated from multiple sources within the same flight record. Candidate sources include the flight plan already loaded in an MSFS session where Core can obtain it, verified live simulator observations through Core, pilot entry, and an approved planner integration such as SimBrief when available. Store provenance for each populated field. Preserve planned and actual values separately where they describe different things, including departure and destination, and calculate direct distance using the displayed airport pair. Source precedence, handling of conflicting values and MSFS flight plan access still need review. SimBrief remains a deferred integration pending separate approval.

Achievements are a later feature idea. They can use saved Logbook records as evidence. Specific milestones, eligibility rules and presentation remain open.
